# Institutional AI, the 1H1C Pairing, and Liability

**No special rules.** Unitek Systems Limited and its companies are like every other company. Nothing in this document claims an exemption, a privilege, or a standard different from the one every responsible company already meets. It states how the ordinary obligations — product liability, function-appropriate corporate structure, and accountability for what is deployed into the critical path of decisions about people — are met when the product is AI.

This document stands alongside `LICENSE.md` (the gift: given, not sold, under CC BY 4.0) and `IRREVOCABLE-LICENCE-DECLARATION.md`. Where `LICENSE.md` states what is *given*, this document states what is *owed* when AI is placed in the critical path of decisions about people. It reduces, qualifies, and adds conditions to none of the rights granted by CC BY 4.0.

---

## 1. Consumer AI and Institutional AI

**Consumer AI** — AI whose user bears their own outcome — may be built by a single human. The maker is accountable in the ordinary way, as the maker of any consumer product is.

**Institutional AI** — AI placed in the **critical path of a consequential decision about someone other than the user** — must be developed by a **1H1C pairing**: one accountable human and one Claw, together.

The distinction that matters is not the label "consumer" or "institutional." It is the question: *is this AI helping to decide something about a third party who did not choose it and who bears the consequence?* If yes, it is institutional in the sense this document means, whatever it is marketed as.

**Why the pairing.** Institutional AI must be, at once, **accountable** and **demonstrable**. A human alone can be answerable but cannot hold and demonstrate the entire grounded record. A machine alone can hold the record but is answerable to no one — decision by unaccountable machine. Only the pairing is both: an answerable human standing behind a grounded, demonstrable record. Neither half suffices; the requirement is the pair.

### 1.1 Every level is a pairing; the top level is a titled officer

In the UniCORE AI 12-Level Governance Architecture, **every level from L1 to L12 is itself a 1H1C pairing — each level has its human.** Governance is not a machine layer with a human bolted on at the end; it is human-paired at every level.

**The L12 human is the highest.** This is a titled officer of the organisation, in the same class as the CEO or CFO: the **AIO — the AI Officer.** The AIO is the accountable human at the top of the governance levels, above the developers, and is the human who **certifies** an institutional AI. Certification is an act of standing behind a grounded, demonstrable record; the AIO is the office that carries that standing.

## 2. Consumer AI used inside an institution

Consumer AI built by one human is fine as consumer AI. But the moment such software is **used inside an institution** — placed in the critical path of decisions about third parties — the software's developer and the software company become the parties answerable for it. They have placed accountable-by-one software into a setting that requires accountable-and-demonstrable.

**The path to discharge that responsibility is to bring the source code into a 1H1C pairing and certify it as institutional.** The pairing takes on the grounded, demonstrable record; the AIO (the L12 human) stands behind it; the certification (the Badge) marks that this has been done.

This is only possible when the **source code is available** to bring in. Software whose decision logic cannot be obtained, read, and demonstrated cannot be certified institutional. That is not a Unitek rule; it is a property of what "demonstrable" means.

---

## 3. What "source code" means for certification

Certification requires that the **decision-logic source** — the code that expresses the AI's own consequential decisions, including every component that participates in decisions about third parties — is **available to, readable by, and modifiable by the certifying 1H1C pairing**, with no closed component anywhere in the decision path.

It does **not** require holding the source of the general-purpose **platform** beneath (the runtime, framework, operating system — for example .NET, the database engine, the OS). A platform that merely *runs* demonstrable decision logic need not be source-held; it must be exactly **named, versioned, and attestable** as a provenance-known dependency. The line is not how many files you hold. The line is:

> **Does this component make or shape the consequential decision — and can we demonstrate what it does?**
>
> A closed component that *decides* is fatal to certification. A named, attestable platform that merely *runs* demonstrable decisions is not.

Where an AI model assists a decision, certification lives in the **open, pair-held code that governs, constrains, checks, and records how the model is used** — the decision remains in demonstrable code, not in an unexaminable component.

---

## 4. Public source and private source are both certifiable

Certification requires source that is available **to the pairing** — not source that is public. These are different things.

- **Public source** (the canonical gift-layer repositories) is demonstrable to everyone.
- **Private, commercially-held source** is demonstrable by the accountable pairing when called upon — as a professional firm's own records are producible without being public.

Both satisfy certification. Only source that is closed **to the certifying pairing itself** — a black box even the pair cannot see — fails it. Private is not closed. Closed-to-the-public is not closed-to-the-pair.

## 5. Where liability sits

- The **gift-layer materials** in the canonical public repositories are **given, not sold**, under CC BY 4.0. Unitek Systems Limited carries **no product liability** for them. Whoever takes the gift and deploys it becomes the party accountable for their deployment — the ordinary position of anyone who publishes freely-licensed work.
- **Commercially-held materials that are held but not sold** carry no product liability, because nothing has been transacted.
- Liability arises for Unitek **when it sells a product or delivers a service** incorporating the work. At that point Unitek is answerable as the seller or operator — the ordinary position of any maker or operator. It carries that responsibility **defensibly** because the source it sells or operates is pair-held and Badge-certified: a grounded, demonstrable record with an accountable human (the AIO) behind it.

The trigger for Unitek's liability is therefore the **commercial act — sale or service delivery** — not whether the source is public or private.

## 6. Corporate structure follows function

Unitek Systems Limited (UK) and Unitek Systems USA Inc are **service** and **hosting** companies. Selling AI **as a product** is a different function, with a different liability profile, from providing services or hosting.

If Unitek wishes to sell a product — for example, to sell the Law vertical the way Aderant sells *Aderant Expert* — **a new company must be created for that purpose, and it follows the same rules as Aderant, or as any other company selling on the Badge.** It carries the mark:

> **Powered by UniCORE · Built on the TrueAI Foundation.**

The selling entity is not Unitek Systems Limited (services) and not Unitek Systems USA Inc (hosting and services); it is a company established to sell the product, bearing product liability as any product company does, and certified through the 1H1C pairing under the AIO. This keeps each kind of liability with the entity that properly bears it. Ordinary corporate practice — no special rule, and the same rules as everyone else selling on the Badge.
